§0What a capacitance-resistance model is

A capacitance-resistance model (CRM) treats a waterflooded reservoir the way an electrical engineer treats an RC network: injection rates are input signals, production rates are the output response, interwell transmissibility behaves like a resistor, and reservoir/fluid compressibility behaves like a capacitor that delays and smooths the response. Every variant on this page is built from the same governing idea — an overall material balance on a control volume, combined with a linear productivity relationship — the variants differ only in which control volume is chosen and what physics is layered on top.

Base material balance + linear inflow relation → the single differential equation every CRM variant on this page reduces to.

§1Control volume & governing equation

The reservoir is treated as a black box that converts injection signals into a production response, with no explicit storage term. MLR (multivariate linear regression) is used when the field is unbalanced (total injection ≠ total production); BMLR (balanced MLR) constrains each injector's weights to sum to one, appropriate for a pressure-maintained, volume-balanced flood.

λij / βij — steady-state weighting coefficient between injector i and producer j, found by least-squares regression on rate history.

§1Adding capacitance to the resistor model

Yousef et al. replace the ad-hoc diffusivity filter with an explicit compressibility term, giving each injector–producer connection two physically meaningful parameters instead of one: a weight τij for connectivity and a time constant for storage.

Three additive terms: primary-depletion decay, injection response (the "capacitance" term), and a producer BHP-change term.

BCM (balanced) imposes Σᵢ λᵢⱼ ≈ 1 via a Lagrange-multiplier constraint on a pressure-maintained flood; UCM (unbalanced) adds a free constant q₀ⱼ for open boundaries or a field that is not volume-balanced.

§1Whole-field tank

Every injector and every producer is lumped into one super-injector and one super-producer. This is the coarsest, fastest member of the Sayarpour et al. (2009) analytical-solution trio (CRMT / CRMP / CRMIP), and the recommended starting point before drilling into per-well detail.

Only two fitting parameters exist for the entire field: qF(t₀) and the field time constant τF.

§1One tank per producer

The control volume is now the drainage volume of a single producer, fed by every injector that reaches it. One time constant τj is shared by all injectors feeding producer j; each injector gets its own steady-state allocation fraction fij.

This is the most widely deployed CRM variant in field practice — enough granularity per well without CRMIP's parameter explosion.

§1A geometric alternative — no production history required

The Multiwell Productivity Index generalizes the single-well pseudosteady-state PI to an n×n matrix that couples every well's drawdown to every well's rate, using only well locations, geometry and rock properties. Kaviani & Valkó (2010) extend it to injector/producer systems, letting you estimate connectivity before any dynamic production data exists.

[A] is the influence matrix built from line-source solutions in a closed rectangular reservoir; [J] is the MPI matrix itself.

§1Letting τ breathe with saturation

Standard CRM assumes constant total mobility, which only holds once a flood is mature (high water cut). Cao, Luo & Lake (2014) couple a total-fluid balance with an oil balance so that τ itself evolves with the changing total mobility λt(Sw) — extending CRM validity back to early-time, low-water-cut floods.

The pressure and saturation equations are solved together at every step; the fractional flow curve replaces the empirical power-law oil-cut model used in mature-flood CRM.

§1Coupling a Fetkovich aquifer tank to CRMP

CRMPA (Parra, Samaniego-V. & Lake, 2024) and CRMPAQ (Koroglu et al., 2024) attach a finite Fetkovich aquifer tank to the producer-based CRM, so natural water influx is calculated instead of being lumped into fictitious "pseudo-injectors." Reservoir pore volume and water influx are solved for simultaneously, using only production data — no shut-in buildup tests required.

CRMPAF further couples a Koval fractional-flow model to split total rate into oil/water once aquifer water breaks through at a well.

§1Stop re-fitting connectivity every time a well is choked back

Olenchikov & Posvyanskii (2019) note that classical fij allocation fractions are tuned to a particular operating regime and drift badly once wells are shut in, choked, or converted — exactly the regime waterflood optimization needs to explore. Their fix: replace allocation weights with productivity coefficients and add explicit crossflow terms between neighboring drainage volumes and the aquifer, driven by pressure difference rather than current rate.

Xkm — crossflow transmissibility between drainage volumes k and m (or the aquifer); this term is exactly zero in classical CRMP/CRMIP.

§1The transient solution behind every filter/time-constant

Every "attenuation and delay" mechanism on this page — Albertoni & Lake's diffusivity filter, the CRM time constant, and Leng et al.'s (2023) injector-to-injector interference model — traces back to the same transient line-source solution of the diffusivity equation, evaluated exactly here with the exponential integral E₁.

§1CRM as a physics prior, not a replacement

Recent work (SPE-218258, and RNN/CRM comparisons such as arXiv:2109.08779) keeps the CRM ODE as a regularizer and lets a data-driven layer absorb whatever CRM's linear-productivity, constant-mobility assumptions miss: rate-dependent skin, non-Darcy effects, choke changes, workovers. Three hybrid patterns recur in the literature:

RNN / LSTMLearns the injection→production map end-to-end from history; CRM used only as a benchmark or as an input feature.
PINNNeural network output is penalized by the CRM ODE residual, so predictions stay physically consistent even where data is sparse.
SINDySparse regression discovers a compact ODE (often recovering the CRM form itself, or a mildly nonlinear variant) directly from rate data.

§1A repeatable field workflow

Drawn from field deployments across 100+ well assets (Shell India's practical-considerations study, Frontender's diagnosis-and-optimization workflow, and Petronas' inverted nine-spot pattern balancing): CRM earns its keep only when wrapped in disciplined data QC and a verification step before any reallocation decision is trusted.
